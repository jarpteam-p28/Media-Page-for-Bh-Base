"Captain William" is William Hobson, a 12-year-old aspiring pilot who, according to ABC News, won people over online with his aviation knowledge and in-flight lingo aboard Delta Air Lines' DREAM Flight in October 2026. The flight is an annual Delta program that takes students on a one-day aviation trip. This year it carried about 160 students from Atlanta to an Airbus aircraft plant in Mobile, Alabama.

## Who is Captain William?

ABC's *Good Morning America* ran two video segments on him, dated [October 22](https://www.goodmorningamerica.com/video/137494069) and [October 23, 2026](https://goodmorningamerica.com/video/137493900). The program describes him as an aspiring pilot who is "winning hearts online." In the first segment he spoke with ABC News' Alex Presha about what sparked his interest in flying.

Most of what is public about him comes from a short local report. [Atlanta News First](https://www.atlantanewsfirst.com/2026/10/20/deltas-dream-flight-sends-160-students-career-focused-trip-atlanta/), which interviewed him on the day of the flight, describes him as a metro Atlanta student who has wanted to be a pilot since he was 7. He told the station his interest began during the COVID-19 pandemic: "I was looking at planes and I was like, I want to do this."

He also told the station, "With Delta Air Lines, I feel like I can conquer a lot."

He is a minor, and the coverage shares little beyond that. This article does the same.

## What happened on the flight?

The flight left Hartsfield-Jackson Atlanta International Airport on Tuesday, October 20, 2026, [Atlanta News First reported](https://www.atlantanewsfirst.com/2026/10/20/deltas-dream-flight-sends-160-students-career-focused-trip-atlanta/). The station said the crew was made up entirely of Black aviation professionals.

According to [Delta's own account](https://news.delta.com/curiosity-career-deltas-dream-flight-continues-build-aviation-talent-pipeline), the students spent the day in Mobile rotating through four activities:

- a tour of the Airbus A320 final assembly line
- aviation challenges at the Old Canteen Center
- drones and flight simulators at Flight Works Alabama
- a look at the advanced manufacturing program at Bishop State Community College

Before flying home, they heard from a panel of Delta and Airbus leaders and were paired with mentors. [Airbus's write-up](https://www.airbus.com/en/newsroom/stories/2026-10-airbus-delta-collaborate-for-the-26th-annual-dream-flight) adds an aircraft-manufacturing scavenger hunt to the list.

Kurt Ford, Delta's managing director of flight training, described the mood on board to Atlanta News First: "It's a little hectic on the plane. The kids are excited."

## What exactly went viral?

ABC News says William showed off "impressive in-flight lingo" during the flight. In other words, he spoke the language pilots and flight crews use on board. The coverage reviewed here does not say which platform the clip spread on, who posted it first, or how many times it was viewed. Those details are not stated here for that reason.

One related detail: Delta's article includes a photo of a DREAM Flight participant making an onboard announcement from the aircraft galley while Delta pilots look on. Delta does not name the student, so it is not confirmed that the photo shows William.

## Why did it strike a chord?

The coverage points to a few reasons.

The first is the story itself. A child who picked a dream at 7, during the pandemic, and five years later got to try it out on a real airliner is an easy story to root for.

The second is who was around him. Delta frames the program around representation. Keyra Lynn Johnson, Delta's chief diversity, equity, inclusion and social impact officer, said in the company's account that "representation can be a powerful catalyst for unlocking potential, especially when students see people who look like them."

The third is that the program has a track record. Two pilots who flew on the very first DREAM Flight, at age 12, now fly for Delta as first officers, according to [Airbus](https://www.airbus.com/en/newsroom/stories/2026-10-airbus-delta-collaborate-for-the-26th-annual-dream-flight). William is the same age they were.

## What is a Delta DREAM Flight?

It is a one-day charter trip that introduces young people to careers in aviation. Delta runs it with the Organization of Black Aerospace Professionals (OBAP), which has partnered on the program since it began, [according to Delta](https://news.delta.com/curiosity-career-deltas-dream-flight-continues-build-aviation-talent-pipeline).

Its roots go back to 1973, when Delta pilot and U.S. Air Force veteran John Bailey began flying charter flights for children, [Airbus reports](https://www.airbus.com/en/newsroom/stories/2026-10-airbus-delta-collaborate-for-the-26th-annual-dream-flight). The first DREAM Flight took off in July 2000. It carried 150 students from Atlanta City Schools to the Smithsonian Air and Space Museum in Washington, D.C., and Delta says an all-Black crew of Delta pilots volunteered to fly it.

The 2026 trip was the 26th. Last year's 25th-anniversary flight went to the Kennedy Space Center. Delta says more than 4,000 young people have taken part over the years, and that the program is designed to be "financially accessible." Bailey was inducted into the OBAP Hall of Fame in 2026.

Not every student wants to fly. Atlanta News First noted that some are weighing careers in the hangar or in engineering instead. Ford told the station the goal is to show kids "careers and opportunities that they may have never seen before."

## What comes next for him?

No outlet has reported any plans beyond the flight itself. What the record shows is simpler: a 12-year-old who wants to be a pilot got a day inside the industry, and a lot of strangers enjoyed watching him love it.
