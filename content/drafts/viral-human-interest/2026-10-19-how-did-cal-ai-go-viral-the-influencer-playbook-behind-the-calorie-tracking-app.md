## What is Cal AI?

Cal AI is a smartphone app that estimates the calories in a meal from a photo. The user snaps the plate, and the app returns calories and macronutrients (protein, carbohydrates and fat). It was founded by Zach Yadegari and Henry Langmack, and Yadegari became known partly because of his age. He was a teenager when the app took off. [VERIFY: founders' names, roles, and Yadegari's age at launch, against a named outlet such as TechCrunch or Business Insider.]

The pitch is speed. Older trackers made users search a database and type in each food. A photo shortcut removes most of that friction.

## How did one influencer post start it?

The Dealroom headline credits one influencer story post as the starting point. A "story" is a post on Instagram or similar platforms that disappears after 24 hours. The point of the framing is that a small, cheap moment of attention can be enough to start a chain of sign-ups and copycat content.

## What was the actual playbook?

Reporting on Cal AI describes a strategy built on volume. Many creators post their own videos showing the app, and the videos look like ordinary content instead of ads. Each clip works as a small, low-cost advertisement, and the ones that catch on get far more reach than they were paid for. [VERIFY: confirm from named coverage that Cal AI paid or seeded large numbers of creators, and how many.]

The mechanism is simple. The app's core trick can be shown in five seconds: photo in, calories out. That makes it unusually well suited to short video.

## Does the app actually work?

Photo-based calorie estimates have a known limit. A camera can identify a plate of pasta, but it can't see the oil in the sauce or judge portion size precisely.
