A large shark wandered into a narrow canal at North Port Waterfront Park in Busan, South Korea, on September 18, 2026, and could not find its way out. Crowds came to watch almost immediately: local authorities put the first weekend at nearly 20,000 visitors, four to five times the park's usual weekend crowd. The shark turned into a meme and an official city mascot. Busan officials say it has now drawn more than 1.7 million visitors.

## Where and when was the shark seen?

The shark was first spotted around 9 a.m. on September 18 in an artificial waterway at North Port Waterfront Park, near Busan North Port ([Korea Herald](https://www.koreaherald.com/article/10881053)). It was seen again at 1:05 p.m. on September 19 ([The Star / Korea Herald](https://www.thestar.com.my/aseanplus/aseanplus-news/2026/09/20/shark-sighting-leads-to-crowds-at-busan-port-in-south-korea)). The canal is less than a mile long and opens into the century-old port ([Boston Globe / New York Times](https://www.bostonglobe.com/2026/10/17/world/wayward-shark-becomes-celebrity-south-korea/)).

One clarification on timing. The weekend that drew nearly 20,000 people was September 19 and 20. The crowds in October were far larger.

## How many people came to see it?

Counts went up fast. Gulf News, citing South Korean media and local authorities, reported about 20,000 visitors over the September 19-20 weekend and about 10,000 more on the Monday. It said the park normally gets around 2,000 a day ([Gulf News](https://gulfnews.com/world/asia/giant-shark-swims-into-busan-canal-then-20000-people-turn-up-1.500685665)). The Korea Herald, citing local media, put that weekend nearer 30,000 ([Korea Herald](https://www.koreaherald.com/article/10881053)). Neither outlet explains the gap.

On the first day of the Chuseok holiday, September 24, about 102,000 people came, according to the Busan Facilities Corporation ([Herald Business](https://biz.heraldcorp.com/article/10884463)). By early October, Busan city authorities estimated more than 800,000 visitors ([Korea JoongAng Daily](https://www.koreajoongangdaily.com/korea/one-shark-draws-52000-people-to-busan-waterfront-in-a-single-day/12904149)). By October 22, Busan officials put the total above 1.7 million ([AP via CP24](https://www.cp24.com/news/world/2026/10/22/a-city-in-south-korea-seeks-new-ways-to-free-a-huge-grey-shark-after-it-slips-back-into-canal/)).

## What kind of shark is it?

Early reports disagreed. Some called it a copper shark, and others a sandbar shark ([The Star / Korea Herald](https://www.thestar.com.my/aseanplus/aseanplus-news/2026/09/20/shark-sighting-leads-to-crowds-at-busan-port-in-south-korea); [Kyunghyang Shinmun](https://www.khan.co.kr/en/article/202609211810007)). On October 6, the National Institute of Fisheries Science said it was a female dusky shark, about 3 meters long. Researchers settled it from close-up footage showing a ridge between the first and second dorsal fins, which copper sharks lack ([Korea Herald](https://www.koreaherald.com/article/10894750)).

Dusky sharks are listed as endangered on the IUCN Red List. They grow and reproduce slowly and can reach 4.2 meters ([Korea Herald](https://www.koreaherald.com/article/10894750)). In South Korea, no attacks on people by the species have been reported ([Kyunghyang Shinmun](https://www.khan.co.kr/en/article/202609211810007)).

The shark's nickname is Bukang-i, a play on "Bukhang," the Korean word for North Port ([Korea Herald](https://www.koreaherald.com/article/10894750)).

## How did it become a meme?

Online reaction took off within days. One meme proposed building a shark-shaped baseball stadium in the North Port area. Other posts joked that the park had become a free aquarium ([Kyunghyang Shinmun](https://www.khan.co.kr/en/article/202609211810007)). A Threads post asking whether anyone was feeding the shark went viral, and commenters started calling the poster a "shark mom" ([Herald Business](https://biz.heraldcorp.com/article/10882307)).

Some of what spread was not true. Kyunghyang reported that a post claiming the shark had given birth was false. Busan City said it had not launched the investigation the post described. The Star noted that some circulating videos were AI-generated, and that claims of a September 20 sighting were unverified ([The Star / Korea Herald](https://www.thestar.com.my/aseanplus/aseanplus-news/2026/09/20/shark-sighting-leads-to-crowds-at-busan-port-in-south-korea)).

Officials joined in. Busan made the shark an honorary city ambassador, a local distillery released a special-edition bottle, and the city's baseball team sold a keychain of its mascot in a shark suit ([Boston Globe / New York Times](https://www.bostonglobe.com/2026/10/17/world/wayward-shark-becomes-celebrity-south-korea/)).

## Were there safety warnings?

Yes, though mostly about crowds. The city sent text messages advising residents not to get close to the water when the shark was nearby ([The Star / Korea Herald](https://www.thestar.com.my/aseanplus/aseanplus-news/2026/09/20/shark-sighting-leads-to-crowds-at-busan-port-in-south-korea)). The Busan Facilities Corporation asked visitors not to watch for more than 30 minutes at a time and said it was managing safety with police ([Kyunghyang Shinmun](https://www.khan.co.kr/en/article/202609211810007)).

Park access was restricted in the evenings starting September 21 ([Korea Herald](https://www.koreaherald.com/article/10881053)). During Chuseok, the city closed the area around the waterway from 6 p.m. to 6 a.m. and sent a public safety alert about severe congestion, urging visitors to take public transit ([Herald Business](https://biz.heraldcorp.com/article/10884463)).

## Why did a shark end up in a Busan canal?

Nobody knows for sure. A National Institute of Fisheries Science researcher said it was very rare for a shark to come that far into the waterway, and that it probably followed prey and got lost ([Korea Herald](https://www.koreaherald.com/article/10881053)). Researchers have also suggested that the canal's curved shape may make the exit hard to find ([Kyunghyang Shinmun](https://www.khan.co.kr/en/article/202609211810007)), and that noise, lights and electric fields from the port may disrupt the shark's senses ([AP via CP24](https://www.cp24.com/news/world/2026/10/22/a-city-in-south-korea-seeks-new-ways-to-free-a-huge-grey-shark-after-it-slips-back-into-canal/)).

The broader context is warming water. The institute says average sea surface temperature in the first half of 2026 was 17.17°C, the highest since 2001, and that warm-water fish and their predators, sharks included, have been moving north ([Korea Herald](https://www.koreaherald.com/article/10881053)). Dusky sharks were first formally recorded in Korean waters in 2019 and have since been documented near Busan and Jeju Island ([Korea Herald](https://www.koreaherald.com/article/10894750)).

## Has the shark made it back to sea?

Not for good, as of the latest reports. Attempts to guide it out failed on September 29 and October 2. A third attempt on October 21 got it into open water. It stayed nearby for hours, then swam under a barrier net that night and returned to the canal ([AP via CP24](https://www.cp24.com/news/world/2026/10/22/a-city-in-south-korea-seeks-new-ways-to-free-a-huge-grey-shark-after-it-slips-back-into-canal/)).

AP reported that the shark had shown no signs of feeding regularly and that falling water temperatures were a concern. Busan authorities and the Oceans and Fisheries Ministry said they were determined to get it back to open water. One institute scientist recommended giving it about a week to leave on its own ([AP via CP24](https://www.cp24.com/news/world/2026/10/22/a-city-in-south-korea-seeks-new-ways-to-free-a-huge-grey-shark-after-it-slips-back-into-canal/)).
