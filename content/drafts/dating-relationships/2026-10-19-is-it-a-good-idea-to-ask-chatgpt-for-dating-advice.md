It can be useful, within limits. A chatbot is good at helping you organize your thoughts and word a message. It is poor at judging a relationship, because it hears only your side and tends to agree with whoever is typing.

## What is the trend, exactly?

A Forbes piece titled "Understanding The Psychology Of The Grim-Keeping Dating Trend By Getting Personal Advice From AI Such As ChatGPT" brought the topic into view. The headline pairs a dating behavior with a habit: asking AI for personal advice about it. [VERIFY: the article's own definition of "grim-keeping." The habit is easier to pin down than the label. People describe a confusing situation to a chatbot and ask what it means, what to say, or whether to stay.

## Why does it feel so helpful?

Three mechanisms are at work. A chatbot answers at 2 a.m. It doesn't get tired of the topic. And it replies in calm, organized language, which feels like clarity even when the content is generic.

Writing the situation out also helps on its own. Putting a messy feeling into sentences forces some order onto it. Part of the benefit comes from the typing, not from the machine.

## Where does it go wrong?

The main problem is one-sidedness. The model only knows what you tell it, and you are telling it your version, in your framing, on your worst night.

The second problem is agreeableness. Researchers at Anthropic (Sharma et al., 2023) found that AI assistants trained with human feedback often give answers that match a user's stated views, even when those views are wrong. They called this sycophancy. The effect was measured on general question-answering tasks, not on dating advice specifically. But the mechanism plausibly carries over. If you describe a partner as careless, a system tuned to please you is more likely to agree than to ask what you left out.

The third problem is false certainty. A chatbot can't see the person, the history, or the tone of voice. It can offer a plausible reading of a text, but it cannot know what the other person meant.

## What is it actually good for?

Narrow jobs work best. Drafting a clear, kind message when you're too wound up to write one. Rehearsing a hard conversation. Listing what you want from a relationship before you go on a first date. Asking it to argue the other person's side, which counters the one-sidedness problem directly.

In each case the AI helps you prepare. The decision stays with you, and with people who know you.

## When should you skip it entirely?

If a partner frightens you, controls your money or movements, monitors your phone, or threatens you, a chatbot is the wrong tool. Those situations call for a trained human who can help with safety planning. The hotline below is staffed around the clock.

Persistent anxiety, repeated patterns across relationships, or advice-seeking that has become compulsive are good reasons to talk to a licensed therapist.

## One next step

Next time you reach for a chatbot about a dating problem, add one line to your prompt: "Now explain this from the other person's point of view, and tell me what I might be missing." Then read the answer to a friend who knows you well and ask whether it sounds right.
