A large shark wandered into a narrow canal at North Port Waterfront Park in Busan, South Korea, on September 18, 2026, and could not find its way out. Crowds came to watch almost immediately: South Korean media, citing local authorities, put the first weekend at about 20,000 visitors. The shark turned into a meme and an official city mascot. On October 22, the Associated Press reported that Busan officials put the number of visitors at more than 1.7 million.

## Where and when was the shark seen?

The shark was first spotted around 9 a.m. on September 18 in an artificial waterway at North Port Waterfront Park, near Busan North Port ([Korea Herald](https://www.koreaherald.com/article/10881053)). It was seen again at 1:05 p.m. on September 19 ([The Star / Korea Herald](https://www.thestar.com.my/aseanplus/aseanplus-news/2026/09/20/shark-sighting-leads-to-crowds-at-busan-port-in-south-korea)). The canal is less than a mile long and opens into the century-old port ([Boston Globe / New York Times](https://www.bostonglobe.com/2026/10/17/world/wayward-shark-becomes-celebrity-south-korea/)).

The weekend that drew about 20,000 people was September 19 and 20. Daily crowds grew much larger in the weeks that followed.

## How many people came to see it?

Counts went up fast. Gulf News, citing South Korean media and local authorities, reported about 20,000 visitors over the September 19-20 weekend and about 10,000 more on the Monday. It said the park normally gets around 2,000 a day ([Gulf News](https://gulfnews.com/world/asia/giant-shark-swims-into-busan-canal-then-20000-people-turn-up-1.500685665)). The Korea Herald, citing local media, put that weekend nearer 30,000 ([Korea Herald](https://www.koreaherald.com/article/10881053)). Neither outlet explains the gap.

On the first day of the Chuseok holiday, September 24, about 93,000 people had visited the park by 6 p.m., according to the Busan Facilities Corporation, and Herald Business put the day's estimated total, including later arrivals, at about 102,000 ([Herald Business](https://biz.heraldcorp.com/article/10884463)). By early October, Busan city authorities estimated more than 800,000 visitors ([Korea JoongAng Daily](https://www.koreajoongangdaily.com/korea/one-shark-draws-52000-people-to-busan-waterfront-in-a-single-day/12904149)). The Korea Herald reported on October 21 that the shark had drawn more than 1.6 million visitors ([Korea Herald](https://www.koreaherald.com/article/10910469)), and AP reported on October 22 that Busan officials put the total above 1.7 million ([AP via CP24](https://www.cp24.com/news/world/2026/10/22/a-city-in-south-korea-seeks-new-ways-to-free-a-huge-grey-shark-after-it-slips-back-into-canal/)).

## What kind of shark is it?

Early reports disagreed. Some called it a copper shark, and others a sandbar shark ([The Star / Korea Herald](https://www.thestar.com.my/aseanplus/aseanplus-news/2026/09/20/shark-sighting-leads-to-crowds-at-busan-port-in-south-korea); [Kyunghyang Shinmun](https://www.khan.co.kr/en/article/202609211810007)). On October 6, the National Institute of Fisheries Science said it was a female dusky shark, about 3 meters long. Researchers settled it from close-up footage showing a ridge between the first and second dorsal fins, which copper sharks lack ([Korea Herald](https://www.koreaherald.com/article/10894750)). AP also gave its length as 3 meters ([AP via CP24](https://www.cp24.com/news/world/2026/10/22/a-city-in-south-korea-seeks-new-ways-to-free-a-huge-grey-shark-after-it-slips-back-into-canal/)), while Korea Herald reports on September 21 and October 21 described it as about 3.5 meters ([Korea Herald](https://www.koreaherald.com/article/10881053); [Korea Herald](https://www.koreaherald.com/article/10910469)).

Dusky sharks are listed as endangered on the IUCN Red List. They grow and reproduce slowly and can reach 4.2 meters ([Korea Herald](https://www.koreaherald.com/article/10894750)). Kyunghyang, writing in September when it still identified the shark as a sandbar shark, said no attacks on people by that species had been reported in South Korea ([Kyunghyang Shinmun](https://www.khan.co.kr/en/article/202609211810007)). None of the sources reviewed here address attacks by dusky sharks in Korea.

The shark's nickname is Bukang-i, a play on "Bukhang," the Korean word for North Port ([Korea Herald](https://www.koreaherald.com/article/10894750)).

## How did it become a meme?

Online reaction took off within days. One meme proposed building a shark-shaped baseball stadium in the North Port area. Other posts joked that the park had become a free aquarium ([Kyunghyang Shinmun](https://www.khan.co.kr/en/article/202609211810007)). A Threads post asking whether anyone was feeding the shark went viral, and commenters started calling the poster a "shark mom" ([Herald Business](https://biz.heraldcorp.com/article/10882307)).

Some of what spread was not true or not confirmed. Kyunghyang reported that a post claiming the shark had given birth was unverified, and Busan City said it had never conducted the investigation the post described. The Star noted that some circulating videos were AI-generated, and that claims of a September 20 sighting were unverified ([The Star / Korea Herald](https://www.thestar.com.my/aseanplus/aseanplus-news/2026/09/20/shark-sighting-leads-to-crowds-at-busan-port-in-south-korea)).

Officials joined in. Busan made the shark an honorary city ambassador, a local distillery released a special-edition bottle, and the city's professional baseball team released a keychain of its mascot, a seagull, in a shark suit ([Boston Globe / New York Times](https://www.bostonglobe.com/2026/10/17/world/wayward-shark-becomes-celebrity-south-korea/)).

## Were there safety warnings?

Yes, though mostly about crowds. The city sent text messages advising residents not to get close to the water when the shark was nearby ([The Star / Korea Herald](https://www.thestar.com.my/aseanplus/aseanplus-news/2026/09/20/shark-sighting-leads-to-crowds-at-busan-port-in-south-korea)). The Busan Facilities Corporation asked visitors not to watch for more than 30 minutes at a time and said it was managing safety with police ([Kyunghyang Shinmun](https://www.khan.co.kr/en/article/202609211810007)).

Park access was restricted in the evenings starting September 21 ([Korea Herald](https://www.koreaherald.com/article/10881053)). By the Chuseok holiday, the city was restricting access around the waterway from 6 p.m. to 6 a.m. daily. On the first day of the holiday it sent a public safety alert about severe congestion, urging visitors to use public transportation ([Herald Business](https://biz.heraldcorp.com/article/10884463)).

## Why did a shark end up in a Busan canal?

Nobody knows for sure. A National Institute of Fisheries Science researcher said it was very rare for a shark to come that far into the waterway, and that it probably followed prey and got lost ([Korea Herald](https://www.koreaherald.com/article/10881053)). Researchers have also suggested that the canal's curved shape may make the exit hard to find ([Kyunghyang Shinmun](https://www.khan.co.kr/en/article/202609211810007)), and that noise, lights and electric fields from the port may disrupt the shark's senses ([AP via CP24](https://www.cp24.com/news/world/2026/10/22/a-city-in-south-korea-seeks-new-ways-to-free-a-huge-grey-shark-after-it-slips-back-into-canal/)).

The broader context is warming water. The institute says average sea surface temperature in the first half of 2026 was 17.17°C, the highest since 2001, and that warm-water fish and their predators, sharks included, have been moving north ([Korea Herald](https://www.koreaherald.com/article/10881053)). Dusky sharks were first formally recorded in Korean waters in 2019 and have since been documented near Busan and Jeju Island ([Korea Herald](https://www.koreaherald.com/article/10894750)).

## Has the shark made it back to sea?

Not for good, as of the latest reports. Attempts to guide it out failed on September 29 and October 2. A third attempt on October 21 got it out of the canal and into the open sea at about 5:50 p.m. ([Korea Herald](https://www.koreaherald.com/article/10910469)). It lingered in nearby waters for hours, then swam under an outer barrier net that night and reentered the waterway. Officials said it was stuck between that net and a second one about 50 meters into the mouth of the canal ([AP via CP24](https://www.cp24.com/news/world/2026/10/22/a-city-in-south-korea-seeks-new-ways-to-free-a-huge-grey-shark-after-it-slips-back-into-canal/)).

AP reported that the shark had shown no signs of feeding regularly and that falling water temperatures were a concern. Busan authorities and the Oceans and Fisheries Ministry said they were determined to get it back to open water. Kim Hyun-woo, a senior researcher at the National Institute of Fisheries Science, recommended giving it about a week to leave on its own ([AP via CP24](https://www.cp24.com/news/world/2026/10/22/a-city-in-south-korea-seeks-new-ways-to-free-a-huge-grey-shark-after-it-slips-back-into-canal/)).
